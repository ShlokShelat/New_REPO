Wave Basics

Sound is a longitudinal pressure wave — compressions and rarefactions travelling through a medium.

Wave Sandbox

λ = v / f
📐

Amplitude

The maximum displacement of a particle from its rest position. Higher amplitude = louder sound.

A ∝ √(Intensity)
🔁

Frequency

Number of complete wave cycles per second (Hz). Higher frequency = higher pitch.

f = 1 / T
📏

Wavelength

Distance between two identical points on consecutive waves. Related to frequency and wave speed.

λ = v / f
⚡

Wave Speed

Sound travels at ~343 m/s in air at 20°C. Faster through denser media like water or steel.

v = f × λ
Longitudinal vs Transverse Motion
Longitudinal (Sound)

Particles oscillate parallel to wave direction — compressions & rarefactions

Transverse (Water / Light)

Particles oscillate perpendicular to wave direction

Resonance & Standing Waves

When a string or air column vibrates at its natural frequency, standing waves form. This is the heart of how all acoustic instruments work.

Resonance in Real Instruments

🎸Guitar

Strings vibrate at harmonic frequencies; the hollow body resonates and amplifies specific overtones.

🎹Piano

Steel strings resonate inside a soundboard. The case acts as a natural amplifier via sympathetic resonance.

🪈Flute

Air column inside the tube resonates at frequencies determined by which holes are open or closed.

🥁Drum

A circular membrane resonates in 2D standing wave patterns — much more complex than a 1D string!

When a sound source moves relative to a listener, the perceived frequency changes — the classic pitch-shift of a passing ambulance.

Doppler Formula
f' = f₀ × (v ± v_L) / (v ∓ v_S)
f' = Observed frequency
f₀ = Source frequency
v = Speed of sound (343 m/s)
v_L = Listener speed
v_S = Source speed

Wave Interference & Beats

When two waves overlap, they add together (superposition). This creates constructive and destructive interference — and the musical phenomenon of "beats."

Constructive Interference

When two waves are in phase (0° offset), amplitudes add together — the sound gets louder.

➖

Destructive Interference

When waves are 180° out of phase, they cancel — producing silence (noise-cancelling headphones!)

🥁

Acoustic Beats

Two slightly different frequencies create a pulsing effect. Tuners use this to detect when two strings are nearly in tune.
